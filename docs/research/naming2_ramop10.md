## 2. What the new objects are

### 2.1 The mail library of the SDK and its work area (WRAM bank 5, `$D000-$DFFF`)

Bank 0F (`lib/mobile/mail.asm`) is the mail library of the Mobile SDK: 13 selectors reached through one dispatcher, `Mail_Dispatch` (0F:4247).  It is entered only through `Mail_DispatchFar` (00:0247) from 12 `farcall` sites in `engine/mobile/` (`pop3_retr.asm`, `pop3_top.asm`, `smtp.asm`), every one right after
`ld a, $05 / ldh [hWRAMBank], a / ldh [rSVBK], a`, it names no WRAM bank register and calls nothing outside itself (4,230 instructions are reachable from the 13 entries, no `jp hl` but the dispatcher's), and all 2,115 of its instruction starts that the replays executed ran under bank 5.  That is the
**invariant L5**: the library runs only under WRAM bank 5.  It is checked by `python3 tools/invariants_check.py` (no hole found by the reader: the only other mention of `Mail_DispatchFar` is a coincidental `$0247` in a table of tile offsets).  The application selects only 8 (once), 6 (8 times), 4, 1 and 2; the selectors 0, 3, 5, 7 and 9-12 are never selected, so what the code says about them is static reading.

* **`wMail_Work`** (W5 `$D000`, 36 bytes) is the work block, and a union: every selector keeps its arguments and its state here and the same byte means different things in different selectors.  The names that hold at every site where they are written are fields: `wMail_InputBank` (+0), `wMail_OutputBank` (+1), `wMail_Selector` (+2: the
  selector in and the result out, but scratch at nine of its eleven uses in the library and the first byte of the argument block of selectors 11 and 12), `wMail_InputPos` (+3..+5 in the parsers 4 and 5: the 6-byte block of the caller, input far pointer then output far pointer; the composer 8 keeps a header index and an item count there), `wMail_OutputStream` (+6..+$0C: SRAM bank, write pointer, bytes still free, size at the start; selectors 4, 5, 8, 9 and 10, shifted by
  one byte in 11 and 12), `wMail_ComposeItem` (+$0F, the composer's item descriptor), `wMail_ErrorFlag` (+$17, the parser error flag of selector 4, a part descriptor in 10), `wMail_ComposeState` (+$23).  Every other byte is written `wMail_Work + offset`.  Eight of these names carry a `CAVEAT` (`wMail_Selector`, `wMail_InputPos`, `wMail_OutputBankVar`, `wMail_OutputStream`, `wMail_ComposeItem`, `wMail_ErrorFlag`, `wMail_ItemListPointer`, `wMail_KeywordValue`: the tool never writes them, they are true only in some selectors).
* **`wMail_TextBuf1`** (W5 `$D024`, 1,536 bytes) and **`wMail_TextBuf2`** (`$D624`, 1,025 bytes) are the two text buffers: `Mail_UnfoldHeader` copies a header value from SRAM into the first (at most `$0400` bytes), `Mail_GetDecodedHeader` turns it into the second with `Mail_DecodeEncodedWords`, the composer builds list items in the first and converts them into the second; the selector 10 (never called) reads chunks of up to `$0E00` bytes into the first, over the second.
  `$D624` of bank 5 is `wMail_TextBuf2` and `$D624` of bank 1 is `wMailSessionBlock`: only the bank in the `DEF`, the proven bank and the file tell them apart (the audit is per bank).
* **`wPromptTextBuf`** (W5 `$D000`, 98 bytes, CONFIRMED): `PromptText_Load` (00:153D) selects bank 5 and copies one of the 20 prompt strings (the longest is 98 bytes) with `CopyString`, then returns `HL = $D000` and `A = 5` to 14 callers that hand the pair to `TextEngine_Run` (executed in 23 scenarios).
* **`wHtmlTextStream`** (W5 `$D000`, 4,096 bytes, CONFIRMED, a `CAVEAT` container) is the output stream of the HTML parser (section 2.4): the same bytes belong to the library at other times, the two never run together.

### 2.2 The sound driver (bank 04, WRAM bank 1 `$D000-$D27F`)

The sound driver is entered only through the 13 stubs of `home/audio.asm` (452 `call`s, every one after `ld a, $01 / ldh [rSVBK], a`), writes no WRAM bank register, and all 1,836 instruction starts of bank 04 that the replays executed ran under bank 1: the **invariant S1**, also checked by `tools/invariants_check.py`.
New names (the driver's own state, all W1): `wSoundDrv_SfxTempoAccum` (`$D008`) and `wSoundDrv_MusicTempoAccum` (`$D00D`), the 16-bit tempo accumulators (CONFIRMED: the accumulator model matches 53,592 frames), `wSoundDrv_ChannelCount` (`$D012`, 38,399,056 decrements at 04:41AC), `wSoundDrv_FadeCounter` and
`wSoundDrv_FadeLevel` (`$D021/$D022`, PROBABLE: the fade step never ran), **`wSoundDrv_Tracks`** (`$D040`, 480 bytes: 8 track records of `$3C`, tracks 0-3 the sound effects, 4-7 the music) and **`wSoundDrv_Channels`** (`$D220`, 96 bytes: 4 channel records of `$18`; CONFIRMED, the init and frame loops with the strides `$3C` and `$18` run 663 and 3.4 million times).  The four `Table_SoundDrv_SfxTrackPtrs` words are `wSoundDrv_Tracks + $00/$3C/$78/$B4`.
`$D03C` is the header track counter only in the two song start routines (04:430A, 04:4374); everywhere else the driver uses it as the dirty-flag mask of a track update (`wSoundDrv_HeaderTrackCount` carries a `CAVEAT`, the eight other sites keep `wRam_D03C`).

### 2.3 The mail screens (WRAM bank 1: `$D400-$D52F`, `$D624-$D638`, `$D726`)

* **`wMailInfo`** (W1 `$D400`, 256 bytes, PROBABLE): the mail information record of the mail server screens, the summary that `Pop3_TopPoll` (bank 54) stages for each mail: date (6 BCD bytes), source label at `+$06`, subject at `+$1B`, sender name or address at `+$C0`; `MailServerMgr_DrawMailFields` (2E:4EBD) and `MailServerMgr_DrawMailDate` (2E:5005) draw it.  It is an overlay of `wEditBodyBuf` and `wEditAddressBuf`, the mail body and
  address staging buffers of the compose, viewer, address book and profile flows (`$D400-$D4FF` is used by both, never together): the mail info flows spell the bytes `wMailInfo + offset`, the others spell them as their buffers, and both `wEditBodyBuf` and `wEditAddressBuf` carry a `CAVEAT`, so those flows are written by hand with their proofs.
* **`wMailTextScratch`** (W1 `$D524`, 29 bytes, PROBABLE, `CAVEAT`): short NUL-terminated texts built or shown by the mail screens: (1) the number strings (a template of full-width zeros whose second byte of each cell is incremented), (2) the result messages (the longest are `MailResult_Txt_SendFailed`, `_ReceiveFailed` and `_CannotReceive`, 29 bytes with the NUL), (3) the 12 date digits, (4) the name and subject texts of the received mail grid,
  (5) **the result text of the connect dialog** (the connection password, PROBABLE): the six 7-byte argument templates that the session flows copy to `wMailSessionBlock` carry the far pointer 01:D524, `ConnectDialog_Run_Accept` (57:4096) writes the typed or stored text (at most 8 characters) and a NUL there, and `MailConnect_Screen` copies the 9 bytes to `$C0A0`, the password source of `Mobile_BeginConnect`.  The same address is also the compose mode (`wMailComposeMode`) and
  the reply record number (`$D525/$D526`): those stay numeric or neutral.
* **`wMailSessionBlock`** (W1 `$D624`, 21 bytes, was declared 10): `+0` the send result (also the address book's mode flag, the dialog mode of the `ConnectDialog_Run` argument block, and the last drawn elapsed seconds of the delete and tidy screens), `+1..2` the received count, `+$0A` the count at the start (`wMailCountAtStart`), `+$0B` the number of mails found and `+$0D` a counter (their increments never ran in a natural scenario: static reading),
  `+$0F` a counter that the tidy loop decrements to 0, `+$11` two saved words, `+$13` the abort flag; at a `ConnectDialog_Run` call the first 8 bytes are its argument block (mode, bank and address of the result text, `$0C` minus the record count).
* **`wAbookListState`** (W1 `$D726`, 3 bytes, PROBABLE): the address book list state: cursor row or slot, re-entry flag, button row (`Abook_Run_List` 2F:7ECC, `AbookList_Run`, `view.asm`, `save_confirm.asm`; ranges observed 0-5, 0-1, 0-2).

### 2.4 The browser and the HTML engine (WRAM banks 6, 4 and 5)

Bank 6 is the browser's work area, selected by `ld a, $06 / ldh [hWRAMBank], a / ldh [rSVBK], a`: `wHtmlAlignStack` (`$D000`, 256: the alignment values of `<center>` and `<div align>`, the push refuses at `$FF`), `wHtmlListStack` (`$D100`, 512: two-byte entries of `<ul>`/`<ol>`), the three `<meta>` value buffers
`wHtmlMetaPppId`, `wHtmlMetaRCode`, `wHtmlMetaDCode` (`$D300/$D340/$D380`, 64 each, PROBABLE: the copy never ran, the readers did; the `d_code` is the error detail only when `r_code` is `ng`), `wBrowserPageTitle` (`$D3C0`, 22: the title record, at most 21 bytes), `wBrowserUrlSave` (`$D400`, 256) and `wBrowserPageUrl` (`$D500`, 256: the URL being navigated to and the URL on display),
`wHtmlLinkPtrList` (`$D600`, now 512 bytes: at most 255 word pointers and the terminator), `wHtmlLinkHeap` (`$D800`, 2,048: the strings of the link table, each a tag byte and a NUL-terminated text, written at `wHtmlLinkHeapPtr` with the limit `$DFFE`; the two Y bytes of a `#` anchor can fill `$DFFE-$DFFF`) and `wDictHistory` (`$DA00`, 768: the mobile dictionary viewer's back stack, six entries of `$80` bytes, over
the link heap).  Bank 4 is the page store (`hHtml_PageBank = 4`): `wHtmlPage` (`$D000`, 4,096: the title, the record count word at `+$15`, 16-byte layout records from `+$20`; the scan pass keeps the image URL heap behind them), `wHtmlImageBaseUrl` (`$DD00`, 256: a copy of the page URL) and `wHtmlImageUrlList` (`$DE00`, 512 word pointers).  Bank 5 holds the parser's output stream (`wHtmlTextStream`, 66,748 stores of 74:4345 in 15 scenarios).
Three of these names have no `ld r16, $Dxxx` operand of their own (the code builds the address from a high-byte immediate): they document the layout.

## 3. How the tool proves the bank (what is new in this pass)

The mechanism is that of `naming2_ramop7.md` to `naming2_ramop9.md`; the pass adds (details in the docstring of `tools/apply_ram_operands.py`, the headers of the two rule files and `STYLE.md`):

* **The bank is proven at the consumer, not at the load.**  A write of a bank register may lie between the load of the pointer and the call (or the dereference): the bank that counts is the one at the consumer (`bank_at(lines, consumer)`), and the replay mask of the consumer instruction is the evidence (the mask of the load only when nothing writes a bank register in between); `--observed` maps the consumer and the dereference lines to their ROM addresses.
  Two sites (`inline_images.asm:23`, `mobile_dictionary_view.asm:278`) load `$DD00` under bank 6 and use it under bank 4: they are named for bank 4.
* **`(direct)` behind a switch and a loop head**: the first instruction that uses the register may sit behind a bank switch and behind a local loop head whose body keeps the bank; the bank is the one at the dereference.  The new outcome **`overrun`** keeps a pointer numeric when an immediate count next to the load exceeds the bytes from the pointer to the end of the object (a wipe or copy over the neighbours: `mailbox_screen.asm:1354`);
  it is a heuristic (it does not see a count in `DE` or `A`, set further up, or an end-address compare), and the 67 looped sites of the tree were read one by one.
* **A local label is crossed when every way into it shows the same bank** (`label_join_bank`): the fall-through, each `jr`/`jp` that names it from above, and the back edges of a loop whose body writes no bank register, calls only routines that keep it and has **no label that is entered from outside the body** (`interior_entries_ok`: a jump into the middle of a loop brings another bank to the back edge; this hole was found by the reader of the tool, reaches no real row, and is closed and tested).  A `call`, a table word or a load of the label's address gives up, as do more than four nested joins.
* **A routine that loads the address of a bank register anywhere** (`ld de, rSVBK`, `ld hl, $FF70`) is refused whole: a later `ld [de], a` would be a bank write that no backward scan can see (the source has none; found by the same reader).
* **`needs = fixed`**: the bank is a property of the code and not of its caller.  The 13 consumer rows of the mail library (`Mail_CopyFromSram`, `Mail_Base64Encode`, ...) use it; each row's proof states the invariant L5, and `tools/invariants_check.py` re-derives it (and S1) from the tree, the ROM bytes and the replays.
* **`a` rows with A = 0**: "the bank in force" holds only for a consumer that `wramx_calls.tsv` lists as `keeps if A=0`; `TextTiles_RenderGrid` hands A to `ReadByteFar`, which has no test for 0 (it would write 0 to both bank registers, which selects hardware bank 1).
* **`; raw` is never rewritten**, in any mode (`--areas`, `--elements`, `--neutral`) and by `tools/apply_manual_sites.py` too (the manual tool wrote two names over `; raw` lines before the readers found it).
* **By-hand rows of a repeated word**: `$D0A3 x2` in the `operand` column of a record names every occurrence of the number on a `dw` line when it occurs exactly that many times.
* **Effects**: 81 new rows in `wramx_calls.tsv`, every call target of the mail library (76) and a few helpers (`Divide16`, `CommTime_*`, `Glyph_IsSjisLeadByte`, `Bmp_Validate`); the 20 `Mail_*_NextPage` rows document the library and are never read (they are called with `call nz|c`, which is no proof).

## 5. Findings and open points

* **Two invariants carry rows.**  L5 (the mail library runs only under bank 5) stands behind the 13 `fixed` consumer rows, the 76 `keeps` rows of the library and the 149 changed lines of the library that no replay executed (122 of them only in the dead selectors or in unreached fragments); S1 (the sound driver runs only under bank 1) behind the 53 respelled `wSoundDrv_*` uses and the four track words.  If either had a hole those sites would lose their proof, so `tools/invariants_check.py` makes it a command: 452 stub calls, 12 `Mail_DispatchFar` sites, the ROM bytes of every far call and the observed masks.
* **An address with two meanings stays numeric or hand-written where the site needs it**: `$D524` of bank 1 (compose mode, the number and text scratch, the reply record number `$D525/$D526`, the connect dialog's result text), `$D400-$D4FF` (mail body and address buffers / the POP3 summary), `$D624` of bank 1 (session block, address book mode flag, the dialog argument block, the seconds cache) and of bank 5 (`wMail_TextBuf2`),
  `$D00D/$D00E` and `$D00F/$D010` of bank 5, bank 6 `$D000` (align stack / the never executed developer image of `dev_test_config.asm`), bank 6 `$DA00` (dictionary history over the link heap), `$D03C` of bank 1, bank 2 `$D000` (tile staging / the debug string scratch).
* **The library's union** was read selector by selector: 1, 2, 4, 6 and 8 are backed by code and replays, 3, 5, 7 and 9-12 only by reading (their formats are internally consistent: 11 and 12 take a 10-byte caller block and `BC` at `+2..+$0F`, 9 keeps a second output at `+$15..+$19`, 10 a part descriptor at `+$17..+$1B`).  PROBABLE is the right level for the fields.
* **A pointer list holds 255 entries**: `wHtmlLinkPtrList` and `wHtmlImageUrlList` are 512 bytes of zero-terminated word pointers; a 256th entry would put the terminator on `$D800` (the first byte of the link heap) or `$E000` (outside the bank).  Emulated by the reader of scope B.
* **`keeps` is still a statement about the shadow**: `Stat_ScrollSplitHandler` (00:0E93) restores the shadow from the real `rSVBK` it read at entry, so a preempted sound bracket would leave `hWRAMBank = 1`; S1 and L5 speak of the real register and are not affected.
* **False references in two data tables** (found by the readers, written as numbers by the commit before this pass): `engine/browser/frame_style_chooser.asm:256-321` is a table of tile offsets in which 29 values were spelled as labels of bank 0 (`dw Rst_08`, `dw Mail_DispatchFar` for `$0247`, ...) by the early symbolisation, and `data/text/comm_error_messages.asm:444` had `dw Rst_20` for `$0020`; none was a reference to those routines, and a search for the references to a routine had to discard them.
* **Still open**: the 123 neutral uses (R2 proposed names for the 121 of the library, all bank 5 by L5, and two immediates that are the high and low byte of `wMail_TextBuf1/2`: they need a second look and a new kind of operand), a name for the reply record number `$D526`, the six `ConnectDialog_Run` template words (`dw wMailTextScratch`), `Html_Layout_ClearAllFloats` (a possible endless retry in a list), the macros for the bank-switch idiom and the sound requests.

## 7. Reproduce

`python3 tools/apply_ram_operands.py --areas wramx --observed --dry-run [--report FILE]` lists what the rules prove (the report has the consumer and the proven bank of every operand); `--neutral --observed` does the same for the neutral names; `--check` audits what is still numeric although a rule proves it.
`python3 tools/apply_manual_sites.py --sites analysis/naming2/ramop10_manual.tsv [--dry-run]` writes (or checks, idempotently) the by-hand rows.  `python3 tools/invariants_check.py` re-derives the invariants L5 and S1.  Tests: `python3 tools/test_ram_operands.py` (84) and `python3 tools/test_manual_sites.py` (7).
